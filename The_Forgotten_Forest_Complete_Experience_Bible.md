# THE FORGOTTEN FOREST
# Complete Narrative, World-Building, Art, Asset, Interaction & Experience Bible

**Purpose:** Definitive creative direction for upgrading the existing Roblox project. This is the single source of truth for the intended experience. Antigravity must inspect the current game, preserve working systems, and upgrade it in place. Do not make a small demo, generic forest, or disconnected collection of mechanics.

---

# 0. THE NON-NEGOTIABLE VISION

The Forgotten Forest must feel like a place that existed long before the player arrived and will continue to exist after the player leaves.

The player should feel small compared with the forest, curious about what lies beyond the next ridge, safe when returning to camp, nervous as daylight fades, rewarded for noticing environmental details, connected to other players through shared danger, and intrigued by a story discovered rather than explained.

This is not primarily a combat game. It is an exploration and survival adventure where combat is one possible response to danger. The forest, its history, paths, sounds, weather, architecture and creatures are the main characters.

**Core emotional rhythm:** wonder → curiosity → unease → preparation → tension → cooperation → relief → revelation → anticipation.

A successful session should contain several of these emotional beats. Do not keep the player in constant danger; contrast is essential.

## Fixed identity

- Genre: cooperative survival, exploration, mystery and atmospheric horror.
- Platform: Roblox; standard R15 avatars; third-person camera.
- Server capacity: 1–12 players.
- Visual direction: stylized low-poly Roblox environment with modern lighting, atmospheric depth, handcrafted landmarks, expressive effects and readable silhouettes.
- PvP disabled in v1.0.
- One continuous map per server. Player progression persists across sessions; the active physical world is server-instance based.
- Tone: mysterious, adventurous, atmospheric and occasionally frightening, not relentlessly scary.
- Monetization: optional cosmetics only; no pay-to-win.
- Target: enjoyable short expeditions and meaningful long-term exploration.

The game must never feel like a flat baseplate with trees, a short corridor disguised as a forest, a lobby with tiny zones, a repetitive click simulator, a dark map where navigation is impossible, a jump-scare collection, or a combat arena with survival bars added.

# 1. THE STORY — THE TRUTH OF THE FOREST

## 1.1 Elderglen before the player

The valley was once known as **Elderglen**. It was not an ordinary forest. Its trees shared an enormous underground root system connected to a living structure buried below the valley: **the Heartroot**.

The Heartroot was not a machine, god or ordinary creature. It was a natural intelligence connecting the forest's living systems. It remembered changes in the valley and reacted to damage. It could influence growth, sound, light and the paths through the woods.

The earliest settlers noticed strange patterns:
- A trail sometimes appeared after a storm and disappeared by morning.
- Trees leaned toward people who were lost.
- Certain clearings remained warm during winter.
- Birds stopped singing before dangerous events.
- Old trees seemed to grow around lost objects.
- Some sounds travelled much farther than they should.
- A person walking alone at night occasionally heard a familiar voice calling from the wrong direction.

The people learned to respect the forest. They built around existing clearings rather than cutting through the oldest groves.

## 1.2 The Keepers and the five Seals

A group of scholars, builders and caretakers became known as **The Keepers**. They studied the forest and developed a way to maintain balance between the settlement and the Heartroot.

They built five structures called **the Seals**:

1. **Root Seal** — stability of the underground root network.
2. **Veil Seal** — boundary between the familiar forest and places that seemed to shift.
3. **Echo Seal** — the forest's ability to preserve and repeat sounds and memories.
4. **Ember Seal** — warmth, light and safe paths through the valley.
5. **Heart Seal** — the central balance of the Heartroot.

The Seals were not simple locks imprisoning a monster. They regulated a living system and prevented the Heartroot's reactions from becoming destructive.

The Keepers built stone observatories, wooden signal towers, underground maintenance passages, lantern posts, a central archive, marked trails, shrines and a great beacon at the settlement.

## 1.3 The disagreement

The Keepers eventually discovered that the Heartroot was weakening. The forest was remembering too much: every injury, fire, abandoned place, fear and voice. Its memory was becoming tangled.

Some Keepers believed the Seals should be strengthened. Others believed the Heartroot needed to be awakened fully so it could heal itself.

Keeper leader **Elias Venn** believed he could repair the Heartroot by connecting all five Seals at once. He was brilliant, compassionate and increasingly desperate. His colleague **Mira Vale** believed the plan was dangerous. She argued that the Heartroot could not be forced to heal; the Seals needed to be restored one by one.

Neither side was purely selfish or evil.

## 1.4 The Long Silence

On the night of the experiment, all five Seals were activated together.

For a few seconds, every lantern in Elderglen burned pale blue. The wind stopped. The river became still. Every bird and insect went silent.

Then the Heartroot responded.

Roots beneath the village shifted. Paths folded into unfamiliar routes. Signal towers rang without being touched. Voices from empty buildings echoed through the streets. The forest's memories began appearing in the physical world as false silhouettes, repeated sounds and places that seemed to belong to another time.

The event became **The Long Silence** because no one heard a natural sound for several minutes.

When sound returned, the village was abandoned, the Keepers were missing, several Seals were damaged, the great beacon was dark, and the forest had expanded into former farmland. Survivors fled. Their descendants eventually treated Elderglen as a legend.

## 1.5 The hidden truth — do not reveal early

The Heartroot did not deliberately attack the village. It was overwhelmed by the forced activation. The Seals partially failed and fragmented the forest's memories into physical phenomena.

The creatures are not all ordinary animals or demons:
- Some are protective responses.
- Some are fragments of old memories.
- Some are living things changed by the forest.
- Some are manifestations of fear and sound.
- Some are guardians whose purpose has become confused.

Elias did not simply die. He became connected to the damaged Heartroot in a way neither he nor Mira understood. The Forest Guardian is partly the remaining will of the Keeper system and partly the Heartroot's attempt to protect itself.

Mira survived and sealed the archive. Her records are scattered around the valley.

The central question is not “How do we kill the forest?” It is:

**Can people and the forest live together again, and what must be sacrificed to restore that balance?**

# 2. THE PLAYER AND OPENING EXPERIENCE

## 2.1 Player role

The player is an expedition member sent by a research and recovery organization after unusual signals were detected near the abandoned valley. The player is not a chosen superhero. They are a capable but inexperienced explorer. Their strength comes from preparation, tools, learning and cooperation.

Avoid a long predefined biography; Roblox players should be able to project themselves into the role.

## 2.2 Opening cinematic — 45–70 seconds, skippable

**Shot 1 — The valley:** wide view of forest-covered mountains. Morning mist moves between ridges. A tiny expedition transport follows a winding road. The forest fills most of the frame. Sound: wind, distant birds and a restrained ambient theme.

**Shot 2 — The warning:** close view of a weathered wooden sign with a Keeper symbol. The transport passes. A small lantern on the sign flickers despite daylight.

**Shot 3 — Arrival:** camera follows the player entering a clearing. Safe Haven is visible in the distance, small against the trees. The beacon is dark; campfire smoke rises.

**Shot 4 — First wrong detail:** a distant silhouette stands on a ridge between trees. It is visible briefly. When the camera returns, it is gone. No loud sting or sudden face close-up.

**Shot 5 — Control:** return to normal third-person gameplay. Objective: “Find the expedition camp.” The Cartographer calls from the camp, not from a disembodied tutorial panel.

Allow skipping. Never replay the cinematic on every respawn.

## 2.3 First ten minutes

**Minute 0–1:** player appears on a path toward Safe Haven. The beacon, smoke and watchtower are visible. No enemies attack.

**Minute 1–2:** meet the Cartographer. Short dialogue explains the failed beacon. Player receives a field journal and basic knife. Cartographer points to the map table.

**Minute 2–3:** open map table; only Safe Haven and nearby woods are revealed. Interact with a supply crate for starter items. Teach interaction through world objects, not a long tutorial panel.

**Minute 3–5:** gather a small amount of wood and stone just outside the camp. Nodes have clear prompts. No hostile creature spawns during this first gathering loop.

**Minute 5–7:** return and repair the beacon. It lights with a warm amber glow. Nearby map section becomes visible. A distant light answers from deep in the forest for one second.

**Minute 7–10:** Mara explains healing and warmth. Cartographer notices a strange signal near Split Oak. Player sees a distant silhouette. Objective becomes “Prepare before sunset.”

The first ten minutes teach the core loop and end with curiosity, not a boss fight.

# 3. THE WORLD AS A PLACE

## 3.1 Geography and scale

Build one continuous valley with a readable central orientation. Initial target footprint: approximately **3,000–3,800 studs across its major dimensions**, measured in Studio. Shape it as an irregular valley, not a square platform. Use mountains, cliffs, dense forest, ravines, rivers and waterfalls as natural boundaries.

Exploration pacing targets:
- A meaningful small discovery every 30–60 seconds.
- A medium point of interest every 2–3 minutes.
- A major landmark or encounter every 5–8 minutes along a primary route.
- Normal walking from Safe Haven to the farthest currently available major objective should take roughly 8–12 minutes, depending on route and hazards.

Do not make the map large by adding empty distance. Every route needs visual interest, resource logic, environmental storytelling or a gameplay reason.

## 3.2 World composition

- South/central valley: Safe Haven and beginning of Whispering Woods.
- West woodland: dense forest, streams, scout camps and hidden clearings.
- Northwest ridge: watchtower, old trail and mountain views.
- North/central valley: Abandoned Village, river crossing and ruined roads.
- Northeast mountain: mine entrance and Crystal Caverns.
- Far north: Ancient Grounds, enormous root structure and Heartroot approach.
- Outer edge: cliffs, mountains, waterfalls, dense forest and ravines.

The player should orient themselves using familiar landmarks from many open locations.

## 3.3 Sightlines and routes

Every region needs a distant landmark, a mid-distance directional landmark, near-field details, a view that communicates world scale, and a path visually leading toward the next region.

Use fog and terrain to reveal the world gradually. Let players glimpse future destinations without reaching them immediately.

Routes should include safe trails, narrow shortcuts, ridge paths, stream crossings, bridges, cave passages, hidden waterfall paths and risky shortcuts. Avoid straight-line corridors. Use bends, elevation and partial occlusion.

Boundaries must be believable: mountain walls, thorn thickets, ravines, strong river currents, collapsed bridges, ancient gates, rockfalls and unstable passages. Communicate why an inaccessible route is closed.

# 4. REGION EXPERIENCE BIBLE

## 4.1 SAFE HAVEN — “A SMALL LIGHT IN A HUGE DARKNESS”

**Emotional purpose:** Safe Haven is the player's emotional anchor. After danger, the camp should feel warm, familiar and comforting. It is a small expedition camp at the edge of an enormous forest, not a giant city or sterile lobby.

### Layout
1. Central Beacon Plaza.
2. Main Campfire.
3. Cartographer's Map Tent.
4. Mara's Medical Tent.
5. Crafting Workshop.
6. Storage Shed.
7. Trader Stall.
8. Notice Board.
9. Watchtower.
10. Sleeping/expedition tents.
11. Perimeter gate.
12. Small overlook facing the forest.

### Visual assets
Canvas tents with seams, ropes and stakes; wooden crates with labels; bedrolls; backpacks; rolled maps; lantern hooks; central stone-and-metal beacon with dark glass core; campfire stones and logs; map tables with compasses and paper; hanging flags; water barrels; medical cot and bandage shelves; workshop tools and scrap; storage chests; notice board; lantern posts; rope fencing; watchtower ladder/platform/telescope; muddy paths and footprints.

### Lighting and sound
Warm amber around fire and tents; cool blue-green forest beyond the perimeter; soft smoke and embers; fire crackle, fabric movement, distant conversation, tools, kettle and wind. Music is gentle and sparse. At night the camp remains readable and welcoming.

### NPC behaviour
**Cartographer:** stands at map table, looks toward approaching player, gives short contextual dialogue, updates objectives and map, occasionally studies a map or points toward a landmark. Never follows player constantly.

**Mara:** near medical tent, checks supplies, prepares bandages, tends lantern, opens medical help. Reacts with concern when a player returns downed where practical.

**Trader:** arranges stock and checks ledger. Shop opens only on interaction. Prices/stock are server-authoritative.

### Camp interactions
**Campfire:** prompt “Warm yourself”. Defined radius restores warmth. Emits heat particles, light and sound. Optional fuel system uses explicit UI showing wood cost and duration; never consumes wood silently. Optional sit emote. Not an instant full heal.

**Beacon:** prompts change by state: “Inspect Beacon”, “Repair Beacon”, “Activate Beacon”. States: dark, damaged, partially restored, active. Repair requires defined resources and short interaction. Activation pulses light, plays sound and reveals map information. May reduce selected threat intensity near camp after story milestones.

**Map table:** opens parchment-like map. Discovered areas are visible, unknown areas obscured. Shows known landmarks, quests and events. Objective selection sets compass direction. Secrets remain hidden until discovered.

**Workbench:** recipe list with ingredients, output, craft duration if any and missing items. Server validates. Short assembly effect. Never consume ingredients if crafting fails.

**Storage:** grid UI clearly labelled “This server session only”. Deposits and withdrawals are atomic/server validated. No duplicate items.

**Medical tent:** supplies and recovery explanations. Healing is balanced, not unlimited free full health.

**Notice board:** active main objective, optional tasks and event notices. Group objectives clearly marked.

**Watchtower:** accessible ladder/steps, meaningful view, telescope highlights a known landmark but not every secret.

Safe-zone boundary is readable through lantern posts, fencing, light and environmental cues. Ordinary creatures cannot enter the protected core. Special story threat must be telegraphed and fair.

## 4.2 WHISPERING WOODS — “THE FOREST IS LISTENING”

**Emotional purpose:** first wilderness. Initially beautiful and inviting; unease emerges through details—a too-quiet trail, branch moving without wind, distant silhouette, sound repeating from the wrong direction.

Visuals: varied tall trees, mossy trunks, roots, ferns, low bushes, sun shafts, streams, shallow ponds, fallen logs, natural stepping stones, mushrooms, rare flowers, dusk fireflies, narrow trails opening into clearings, low-ground fog.

### Points of interest

**Split Oak:** huge lightning-split tree; one half dead, one half alive with roots. Keeper symbol inside. First important environmental clue and small cache.

**Abandoned Scout Camp:** two collapsed tents, cold firepit, broken compass, supplies. Journal page mentions footsteps around the camp. Limited server-controlled loot. At night, a distant sound may occur.

**Old Wooden Bridge:** crosses stream/ravine. Aged planks and rope rails; some damaged but main crossing safe. Repair may open shortcut. No unstable physics.

**Silent Pond:** still pool with reeds. Insects become unusually quiet nearby. Reflection may briefly appear delayed as a controlled visual effect. Hidden clue can appear after weather event.

**Keeper Shrine:** small stone structure with carved symbol and three interactable components. Activation reveals short memory or map clue; not a generic loot box.

**Lookout Rock:** elevated view of village clock tower; teaches landmark navigation and contains a small discovery.

Resource distribution: wood near trees/fallen logs, stone near rocky slopes/streams, herbs in sunny clearings, cloth/rope at camps, food at designated gathering spots. Rare resources are deliberately placed.

Watcher is seen at distance first. Stalker is introduced later, after light and basic weapons are taught. First hostile encounter has a clear escape route.

Environmental clues: trail marker points wrong way; Keeper symbol underneath bridge; old expedition tags in bark; footprints stop abruptly; lantern post lit without fuel.

## 4.3 ABANDONED VILLAGE — “A PLACE THAT LEFT IN A HURRY”

**Emotional purpose:** make the player wonder who lived here, what they were doing and why they left.

Layout: main entrance gate, central square, clock tower, houses along winding road, workshop, inn/meeting hall, well, Keeper archive, chapel/observatory, rear garden/fields, hidden underground route.

Use a small modular house family with varied roofs, damage, interiors, props and lighting. Do not make every house identical.

Visuals: timber-and-stone houses, broken shutters, overgrown roofs, worn signs, empty carts, cracked barrels, abandoned toys, household objects, old notices, stopped clocks, roots through floors, dust in light, broken lanterns, mossy wells, collapsed fences, cloth caught on branches.

**Clock Tower:** visible landmark; clock stopped at Long Silence. Inspect mechanism; later restore gears to reveal clue or change village event. It is functional story architecture.

**Village Square:** dry fountain, notice board, abandoned cart, central five-Seal symbol. At night, faint echo of former village activity may be heard.

**Old Inn:** travel supplies, personal notes, hidden compartment, fireplace with Keeper token. Do not fill every drawer with loot.

**Workshop:** tools, broken machinery and repairable mechanism. Teaches Keeper practical technology and may unlock recipe.

**Well:** side passage discovered below; safe readable route and clear return path; small clue room, not arbitrary maze.

**Keeper Archive:** initially locked. Requires three evidence pieces or clue sequence. Unique seal mechanism. Inside: maps, journals, valley model and records about Long Silence. Major story reward.

### Three Lantern Puzzle
Three lanterns have distinct symbols. A nearby mural shows the order. Player lights them in sequence. Correct order opens side room; incorrect order resets after short delay with subtle cue. The puzzle reveals a Keeper record. Teach through observation, not arbitrary code guessing. Solo play must remain possible.

Mimic introduced through sound/silhouette. Stalker patrols outskirts. Do not fill village with constant enemies; quiet exploration matters.

## 4.4 CRYSTAL CAVERNS — “BEAUTY WITH A COST”

Emotional purpose: beautiful and dangerous. Rare resources reward players, but warmth, visibility and navigation become harder.

Visuals: large underground chambers, layered rock walls, restrained glowing crystals, underground river, natural stone bridges, mining platforms, old equipment, roots penetrating rock, light shafts, cold mist.

Assets: modular cave walls/ceilings, pillars, stalactites/stalagmites, crystal sizes, broken mine carts/rails, wooden supports, rope bridges, tools, waterfall, glow particles, cave lanterns, rubble, Keeper machinery.

Gameplay: pickaxe crystal nodes; some areas need lantern/advanced light; cold pockets reduce warmth; unstable rock has visible warnings and escape windows; rare materials for advanced crafting; sound-sensitive creature encounter; discover roots extending far below village.

## 4.5 ANCIENT GROUNDS — “THE FOREST REMEMBERS”

Emotional purpose: ancient, quiet and significant. No generic ruin spam. Every structure implies a purpose.

Visuals: giant roots, broken stone circles, buried statues, pale mist, restrained supernatural light, trees through architecture, ancient mechanisms, huge root/tree silhouette, subtle pulsing ground patches.

Gameplay: multi-player mechanisms, advanced exploration, rare fragments, puzzles combining earlier clues, Forest Guardian encounter and revelations about Elias, Mira and Heartroot.

Boss arena: large natural clearing surrounded by roots and broken Keeper structures. Clear boundaries, safe positions, visible root anchors, cover/dodge lanes, no lethal invisible edges, readable phase lighting and fair return/retry route.

# 5. MAIN GAME LOOP

1. Prepare at Safe Haven: inspect objective, equip weapon/tool, craft supplies and check map.
2. Choose expedition: travel to a known location or investigate a signal.
3. Explore: gather resources, inspect landmarks, find clues and discover routes.
4. React: respond to weather, creatures, warmth, sanity and time.
5. Cooperate: revive teammate, activate mechanism, share route or defend objective.
6. Decide whether to return or push further.
7. Recover and progress: craft, record discoveries, unlock route, complete quest and prepare again.

Meaningful risk/reward decisions: return with supplies or continue; cross ravine before sunset; use last flare now; investigate signal; revive teammate or retreat; take safe trail or fog shortcut.

Do not force players back to camp every few minutes. Allow longer expeditions through crafted supplies, shelters and unlocked routes.

# 6. PLAYER SYSTEMS AND FEEL

Starting values:

| Attribute | Value | Intended feel |
|---|---:|---|
| Health | 100 | A few mistakes survivable; repeated danger matters |
| Stamina | 100 | Sprint useful, not unlimited |
| Warmth | 100 | Environment matters without constant punishment |
| Sanity | 100 | Supernatural exposure changes atmosphere |
| Walk speed | 16 | Familiar Roblox baseline |
| Sprint speed | 22 | Noticeably faster |
| Inventory | 20 slots | Useful expedition capacity |
| Downed timer | 30 sec | Rescue opportunity |

**Movement:** preserve R15 avatar and appearance. Walk 16, sprint 22, drain 12 stamina/sec, regenerate 18/sec after 1 sec without sprinting. Exhaustion after full depletion is brief/fair. No idle drain. Prevent toggle exploit. One authoritative movement service. Responsive feel; surface-specific footsteps, landing sounds and sensible animation transitions. Do not add complex parkour without a map need.

**Health:** maximum 100. Damage from creatures, selected falls, traps, hazards and bosses. Every attack has wind-up, readable area and response. Ordinary enemies do not one-shot healthy players. Healing items have clear values, animations and cooldown. Server owns health/damage.

**Warmth:** decreases in cold caves, heavy rain, exposed night areas and high places. Recovers near fire, shelter, heat and warm clothing. Low warmth creates cold breath/subtle effects; critical warmth causes gradual damage. Beginner area should not be unfairly cold.

**Sanity:** decreases near supernatural events, Mimic encounters, ruins and prolonged isolation in dangerous regions. Recovers in Safe Haven, near teammates or calming places. Effects: distant whispers, false lights, silhouettes, mild distortion and altered ambience. Never disable controls, invert movement or create unavoidable damage. Provide reduced-distortion setting.

**Status effects:** use a shared framework with source, severity, duration, stack limit, refresh and removal rules:
- Cold: warmth falls faster.
- Bleeding: small damage over time until treated.
- Exhausted: sprint temporarily unavailable.
- Dazed: brief movement penalty, never total control loss.
- Marked: creature tracks player briefly.
- Calmed: sanity loss reduced.
- Protected: temporary protection from a defined hazard.

**Downed/revive:** at zero health, enter downed state, start 30 sec timer, show teammate prompt. Revive takes about 4 sec. Cancel if rescuer moves away/is interrupted/target state changes. Successful revive restores small health and brief protection. Expiry respawns at camp/checkpoint. Show timer and rescuer.

**Death:** lose target 20% of eligible carried resource value. Keep XP, levels, permanent unlocks, achievements, paid cosmetics and protected quest items. Apply exactly once with unique transaction/death ID. Show loss summary. No loss due to server crash. Define disconnect-while-downed rule.

# 7. WEAPONS, TOOLS AND THEIR FEEL

Combat is defensive survival, not a shooter. Weapons feel distinct but do not dominate exploration.

| Weapon | Damage target | Range | Cooldown | Identity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Field Knife | 12 | 5 studs | 0.55 sec | Fast starter defence |
| Wooden Spear | 18 | 9 studs | 1.0 sec | Reach/interruption |
| Hatchet | 20 | 6 studs | 1.1 sec | Gathering and combat |
| Keeper Blade | 28 | 6 studs | 0.8 sec | Advanced story weapon |
| Hunting Bow | 22 | Projectile | 1.3 sec | Quiet ranged attack |
| Flare Launcher | 5 direct | Projectile | 3.0 sec | Light/disruption |
| Signal Flare | 0 | Throw range | One use | Mark/distraction |

These are initial balance targets, tune after playtests.

**Field Knife:** compact worn steel blade, wrapped dark handle, simple sheath; short quick slash; restrained metallic swish; starter defence; cannot harvest trees/ore.

**Wooden Spear:** handmade shaft, bound grip, sharpened tip; thrust with short recovery; keeps creature at distance and can interrupt basic Stalker if timed; weak against armoured/boss phases.

**Hatchet:** compact field axe; deliberate chop; primary wood tool and moderate melee weapon; tree has visible damage stages and server-side depletion; ineffective against stone/crystal.

**Keeper Blade:** old ceremonial blade with etched symbols, restrained glow; controlled strike; advanced story artifact; subtle response near mechanisms; does not solve puzzles or trivialize boss.

**Hunting Bow:** practical wooden bow and quiver; draw/aim/release/recovery; finite arrows; recover only valid landed arrows; server controls projectile/hit validation.

**Flare Launcher:** small signal device, not a military firearm; illuminates and briefly disrupts selected creatures; limited ammunition; no permanent boss stun.

**Signal Flare:** marks a location or signals teammates; some creatures may investigate; one-use with clear throw preview and server validation.

Tools: hatchet for wood; pickaxe for stone/metal/crystals; knife for selected small harvesting/defence; lantern for light; rope for approved traversal; repair kit for selected structures; compass for direction; journal for clues. Each node declares its correct tool.

# 8. CREATURE EXPERIENCE BIBLE

## The Watcher — “You are not alone”

Purpose: unease without forced combat. Tall thin silhouette, dark simple shape readable against mist, no detailed face at long range, unnatural stillness. Selects observation point, appears between trees/ruins/on ridge, faces player briefly, disappears when approached/strongly lit/watched too long, may leave clue or subtle sound. Never repeatedly spawns in view. No normal health bar.

## The Stalker — “Something is following you”

Purpose: fair physical danger. Lean creature with long limbs and forest-worn surface; distinctive torchlight silhouette; weighted animation; no gore.

AI states: hidden/dormant → patrol → investigate sound → detect → chase → wind-up → attack → search last position → retreat/return.

More likely to target isolated players. Investigates loud gathering/running/flare. Readable wind-up. Can be interrupted by spear or selected light. Respects safe boundary. Never teleports beside players. Bounded PathfindingService recalculation, not every frame. Limit active creatures.

## The Mimic — “The sound is wrong”

Purpose: make players question what they hear/see. Rare distant silhouette with slightly wrong proportions/movement; never imitate a specific player avatar. Creates fictional calls/footsteps/environmental sounds, may appear at edge of visibility and lure player toward risky route. Controlled effects, not unfair teleport attacks. Counterplay: teamwork, light, noticing inconsistencies, later detection tool. Never impersonate real users, their voices, Roblox staff, moderation or system UI.

## Forest Guardian — “The protector that forgot”

A root-and-stone giant with Keeper structures embedded in its body and restrained glowing lines. Slow, heavy movement. Arena responds to its actions.

Phases: awakening; telegraphed Root Sweep; Guardian Pulse with visible safe zones; cooperative Root Anchors; exposed-core damage window; recovery with changed pattern; story resolution.

Scale for group size. No unavoidable one-shots or attacks that push players off-map. Server owns phases, damage and rewards. Not an endless damage sponge. Encounter reveals story.

# 9. INTERACTION DESIGN — EVERY OBJECT HAS A PURPOSE

Every interactable requires unique ID, visual identity, prompt, range, required tool/item/condition, hold duration, server validation, state changes, reward/consequence, cooldown/reset, feedback, multiplayer claim rules, accessibility and performance notes.

## Resource nodes

**Tree:** hatchet; prompt “Chop Tree”; timed chops; wood chips/sound; intact → chipped → damaged → depleted/stump; wood reward; server cooldown/regrowth; no unpredictable falling physics.

**Stone deposit:** pickaxe; “Mine Stone”; crack stages, chips and mining audio; stone and occasional metal scrap; depleted state and cooldown.

**Herb patch:** “Gather Herbs”; short animation; leaves move and plant becomes harvested; regrows after delay.

**Mushrooms:** inspect/gather; food, crafting or quest items; journal records known types; use fictional game rules, not real-world mushroom advice.

**Crystal cluster:** pickaxe; glow, mining sound and visible cracks; rare crystal reward; dim depleted state; long cooldown/event respawn.

**Animal materials:** prefer shed materials, nests or abandoned remains; avoid unnecessary graphic animal harm.

## Loot

Supply crate, abandoned backpack, Keeper cache, village cabinet, rare sealed chest. Server selects loot. Unique object identity, one-time/cooldown state, no simultaneous duplicate claims, short opening animation, region/risk-appropriate loot, quest items protected from death loss.

## Doors and mechanisms

- Door: hinge animation, correct collision, no clipping.
- Locked door: states required clue/key/quest; clear feedback.
- Gate: lever/power/item/group requirement; collision changes only when safely open.
- Lever: visible server-owned on/off state; spam cannot bypass.
- Pressure plate: stable server-side detection, no physics-jitter spam.
- Puzzle device: partial feedback, server-side state, fair reset.
- Beacon: changes light, sound, map knowledge and selected world conditions.

## Environmental props

- Lantern: lit/unlit/broken states, controlled light and optional creature response.
- Campfire: heat, smoke, sound, light, explicit fuel UI if used.
- Bridge: stable collision, repair interaction, safe rails.
- Rope: approved anchor points, consistent physics.
- Water: readable depth/crossing rules, no invisible lethal zones.
- Cave entrance: strong silhouette, rock framing and lighting transition.
- Journal page: readable text, records after server validation.
- Collectible: clear effect, category and duplicate prevention.
- Notice board: active quests/events.
- Map marker: reveals only after discovery.
- Telescope: shows distant known landmarks, not all secrets.
- Clock mechanism: inspectable, repairable, story-linked.
- Old radio/signal device: fictional recording after correct interaction; never calls real emergency services.
- Statue: rotates/lights/reveals clue if interactive; no decorative false prompt.

## Object state standard

Examples:
- Resource node: `Available → BeingInteractedWith → Depleted → Cooldown → Available`
- Door: `ClosedLocked → ClosedUnlocked → Opening → Open → Closing`
- Beacon: `Inactive → Damaged → Repairing → Repaired → Activating → Active`

For each object define ID, model, state attributes, allowed interactions, range, required item/tool, hold duration, server validation, transition rules, reward, reset/persistence, VFX/audio, multiplayer contention, accessibility and performance. If two players interact at once, only one valid transaction occurs unless group interaction is intentional.

# 10. INVENTORY, CRAFTING AND ECONOMY

Inventory: 20 slots; common resources stack to 50; crafted consumables to 10; rare quest items to 1 unless specified. Equipment slots: weapon, tool, lantern/utility, clothing. Simple mobile hotbar. UI shows name/icon/quantity/description/action. Server owns inventory. Clear full-inventory feedback; never silently delete.

Resources: wood, stone, herbs, food, cloth, metal scrap, rope, animal materials, crystals, ancient fragments, rare minerals and water. Each definition needs ID, display name, icon, stack limit, description, rarity, uses, trade value if any, death-loss eligibility and quest-protection status.

| Recipe | Ingredients | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Torch | 2 Wood + 1 Cloth | Portable basic light |
| Healing Potion | 2 Herbs + 1 Water | Restore health |
| Wooden Barricade | 5 Wood | Temporary defence |
| Basic Trap | 3 Wood + 2 Rope | Briefly slow basic creature |
| Advanced Lantern | 2 Crystals + 3 Metal Scrap | Strong light/detection |
| Warm Clothing | 3 Cloth + 2 Animal Materials | Reduce warmth loss |
| Basic Tool | 2 Wood + 2 Stone | Starter utility |

Crafting is server-validated. Remove ingredients only after successful craft. Ensure output capacity or safe destination. Prevent double-submit. Show required/missing materials. Keep basic survival affordable.

**Forest Coins:** common quest/exploration reward; basic supplies and cosmetics.  
**Ancient Tokens:** rare discoveries/events/story milestones; mainly cosmetics and special non-pay-to-win collectibles.

# 11. DAY/NIGHT, WEATHER AND EVENTS

## 11.1 Twenty-minute cycle

| Phase | Duration | Mood |
|---|---:|---|
| Morning | 3 min | Calm, hopeful |
| Afternoon | 5 min | Best exploration |
| Sunset | 2 min | Beautiful but uneasy |
| Night | 7 min | Dangerous and mysterious |
| Dawn | 3 min | Relief and reflection |

Server controls phase; clients synchronize.

Morning: soft light, dew, birds, lower threats.  
Afternoon: clear visibility and exploration.  
Sunset: orange horizon, longer shadows, birds return to nests, warning and distant creature sounds.  
Night: deep blue/green lighting, moonlight, lantern pools and mist. Not pitch black. Threats/events rise; camp visible from distance.  
Dawn: fog lifts, threats retreat, birds return and music softens.

## 11.2 Weather

Light rain changes leaf/water sound and surfaces. Heavy rain reduces visibility and increases warmth loss in exposed cold regions. Ground fog settles in valleys. Wind affects foliage/flags/particles. Rare Fog Surge is supernatural. Transitions are gradual, synchronized and performance-aware.

## 11.3 World events

- **Strange Signal:** distant beacon-like signal; locate, inspect, decide whether to activate; clue or small encounter.
- **Crystal Bloom:** selected crystal clusters glow and become harvestable temporarily.
- **Fog Surge:** fog spreads; landmarks remain visible; Mimic may appear.
- **Abandoned Caravan:** temporary supplies, journal and story location; creature may investigate noise.
- **Creature Migration:** tracks and movement between regions; players can observe, avoid or follow.
- **Ancient Awakening:** distant root movement, low sound and sky change; hints at Heartroot, not instant final boss.

Events have warning/active/end/reward states, are server-selected, do not spawn on players, break quests or block only route.

# 14. ART ASSET BIBLE

The game must use one coherent asset family. Assets should look as though they belong to the same world.

## 14.1 Environment families

**Trees:** tall straight, wide old, lightning-split, dead, young sapling, twisted supernatural, hollow, fallen log and root cluster. Each family needs silhouette/size variation.

**Ground/foliage:** grass clumps, ferns, moss, bushes, flowers, mushrooms, fallen leaves, roots, fallen logs, pebbles, mud patches, footprints and trail markers.

**Rocks:** small stones, boulder clusters, cliff walls, overhangs, cave walls, pillars, broken masonry and crystal-bearing rocks.

**Water:** stream sections, pond edges, waterfall, shallow crossing, underground pool, foam/splash effects and reeds.

**Architecture:** expedition tents, cabins, village houses, workshop, inn, clock tower, watchtower, stone shrine, Keeper gate, mine entrance, cave supports, ancient statues and root-covered ruins.

**Props:** crates, barrels, sacks, tools, lanterns, candles, tables, chairs, shelves, books, maps, paper notes, compasses, backpacks, carts, broken wheels, ropes, signs, flags, bedrolls, medical supplies, cooking pots, gears, keys and tokens.

## 14.2 Character assets

Cartographer, Mara, Old Keeper, Stalker, Watcher, Mimic and Forest Guardian. NPCs use compatible R15 rigs where appropriate. Maintain consistent scale and style. Do not mix realistic high-poly characters with simple low-poly surroundings. Animations are subtle and readable.

## 14.3 VFX assets

Fire embers, camp smoke, fireflies, dust, falling leaves, rain, fog, crystal glow, beacon pulse, Keeper symbol glow, root movement, creature detection cue, hit impact, weapon swing, healing, revive and boss phase indicator. Effects are restrained; no giant particle burst for every small interaction.

## 14.4 Animation assets

Idle, walk/run/sprint where needed, gather/chop/mine, weapon attack by family, bow draw/release, interact/repair, open chest/door, revive, downed, heal/use item, NPC idle variations, creature patrol/chase/attack/retreat and boss phase transitions. Use animation priorities correctly; do not break default avatar movement.

## 14.5 Asset sourcing rules

Use original assets or assets permitted by Roblox licensing/usage terms. Never rip assets from other games. Never insert untrusted free models with unknown scripts. Inspect imported models for Scripts, LocalScripts, ModuleScripts, remotes and suspicious code. Maintain source/permission notes. If an asset is unavailable, create a consistent placeholder and document it. Avoid excessive mesh complexity and oversized textures.

Maintain `ASSET_REGISTER.md`: asset name/ID, type, source/creator, permission/licence, usage location, script contents check, performance notes and replacement status.

# 15. TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION RULES

## Authority

Server owns health/damage, stamina and survival effects, inventory, item definitions for validation, crafting, currency, XP/rewards, quests, resource depletion, loot, creature state/attacks, revive/death, persistence, world event selection and phase.

Client owns input, UI, camera effects, local cosmetic effects and non-authoritative presentation. Client requests; server validates and applies.

## Remotes

Validate argument type, length, range, distance, player state, cooldown, target, ownership and frequency. Never accept client-supplied damage, prices, rewards or item definitions. Rate-limit every gameplay remote.

## Persistence

Use DataStoreService with protected calls, retries, rate limits, autosave and shutdown handling. Version schemas. Never replace valid saved data with defaults after failed read. Persist permanent progression, not session storage unless designed.

## Performance

Configure StreamingEnabled for large world. Anchor static geometry; use simple collision; avoid excessive dynamic lights, transparent overlap and particles; limit active NPCs/events; avoid expensive server loops; do not pathfind every frame; use region activation; profile with MicroProfiler; test mobile/low-end devices; use collision groups.

- Partial systems.
- Broken systems.
- Missing systems.
- Actual map dimensions.
- Measured travel times between landmarks.
- Current asset inventory.
- Architecture/dependencies.
- Risks/blockers.

Do not claim a feature exists unless inspected.

## Phase B — Gap analysis

Create `EXPERIENCE_GAP_ANALYSIS.md`. Compare current game against every section. Status:
- PASS — implemented and tested.
- PARTIAL — exists but misses target.
- FAIL — broken/materially wrong.
- MISSING — not implemented.
- BLOCKED — genuine dependency/permission.

Include evidence, severity and priority.

1. Measure current map.

4. Connect regions with walkable paths.
5. Add elevation and natural boundaries.

First map milestone is a coherent navigable world, not decorative asset quantity.

**First impression:** opening establishes vast forest and mysterious camp; immediate objective is clear; world appealing but not noisy; no hostile encounter overwhelms tutorial.

# 18. FINAL CREATIVE TEST

Before adding a feature, ask:
1. Does this make Elderglen feel real?
2. Does it create curiosity or a meaningful decision?
3. Does it fit the Heartroot/Keepers story?
4. Does it create a memorable visual, sound or interaction?
5. Is it understandable without a long tutorial?
6. Is there fair counterplay?
7. Does it work for groups and remain reasonable solo?
8. Can it run on Roblox mobile?
9. Does it add depth rather than clutter?
10. Would a player remember this place or moment?

If not, do not add it just because it is easy.

**The Forgotten Forest is a journey through a place that remembers.** The goal is not simply to collect resources and defeat a boss. It is to understand the forest, learn why it changed, decide how to respond and build a reason to return.

The final feeling should be:

*“I thought I was exploring an abandoned forest. I slowly realized the forest was exploring me.”*
